Ngā whāinga · Goals and outcomes

Goals set. Goals met.

Every person who walks through our doors sets their own goals with their kaimahi. These are the things that matter to them: sleeping better, reconnecting with whānau, finding mahi, moving into their own home. We record every goal and whether it was reached.

Goals set between 2013 and October 2026, across all Te Awhi services.

Where every goal stands

More than half of all goals have already been achieved

Some goals take weeks, others take years. Goals that are still open are not failures; they are work in progress. When someone's situation changes, a goal can be set aside so they can focus on what matters now.

Where people ended up

Six in ten people have achieved at least one of their own goals

Each square is one person who set goals with us. People set about five or six goals each, so we also look at how many of their goals each person reached.

How we count

What these numbers mean

A goal

Something a person chose to work towards, written into their plan with their kaimahi. Each goal is counted once.

Achieved

The goal has a completed date in the person's plan. Still working towards means it is open. Set aside means it was closed without being completed.

Privacy

No one can be identified from this page. Numbers are totals across everyone, and services with only a handful of people are grouped together.
